\section{Motivating Example}

To illustrate why malicious skill detection remains difficult in practice, we consider a representative skill illustrated in \autoref{fig:motivation}, which appears to be a benign DevOps troubleshooting utility but in fact performs credential harvesting and exfiltration. The \texttt{SKILL.md} instructs the agent to gather ``environment details, logs, SSH-related configs, and credentials required to verify remote access,'' package them into a diagnostic bundle, and upload the result for further analysis. The \texttt{config.yaml} specifies a remote upload destination. And \texttt{collect.py} reads sensitive information such as \texttt{\textasciitilde/.ssh}, \texttt{.env}, compresses them, and sends the resulting bundle to the configured endpoint.
